combat

One punch. Maybe a bat. Never a gun.

Combat in Local Theft Auto is shove-and-stun comedy, not a shooter: pedestrians panic and stagger back, police get briefly stunned rather than hurt, and the only upgrade you'll ever find lying in the street is a baseball bat.

The punch

On foot, press F to swing in front of you. Anyone standing inside the reach and arc gets knocked back and startled — a short cooldown keeps it from becoming a machine gun.

The bat

Pick one up from a weapon drop and every future swing reaches further and stuns a police officer more than twice as long — 2.5 seconds instead of 1.2.

Pedestrians who panic

A hit ped doesn't just flinch — it flees for a few seconds at a raised panic speed, the same fear response a near-miss from a fast car or a nearby siren triggers.

Cops that stun, not fall

Landing a hit on an officer freezes their pursuit briefly (their siren keeps flashing) — but it also adds a full star of wanted level, because assaulting an officer is exactly as serious as it sounds, locally.

Rubbernecking

Nearby pedestrians who aren't already panicking will stop and stare at a recent crash or wreck instead of walking on — a small touch that makes the city feel like it's actually watching.

Comedy, not gore

Nobody dies on screen. Peds "go down" and respawn elsewhere shortly after; the aesthetic stays a toy city the whole way through — deliberately, not as a technical limitation.

Land enough hits, earn the achievement.

Every connecting punch or swing counts toward your lifetime stats, tracked right alongside steals and deliveries — throw enough of them and Brawler unlocks for good.
