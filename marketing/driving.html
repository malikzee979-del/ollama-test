vehicles & driving

Every car drives a little differently.

Cars aren't interchangeable props. Each one rolls its own acceleration, top speed and grip the moment you steal it, on top of drift-on-handbrake physics, real damage, and lane-disciplined traffic that actually reacts to you.

Handling that varies per car

Acceleration, top speed and grip each roll independently on theft — an owner comment ("this one's got a tune", "floaty suspension", "feels quick") hints at what you just took, before you've even pulled away.

Drift on the handbrake

Hold space and grip drops hard: the car keeps its momentum while you point it somewhere new, laying skid marks whenever lateral speed and total speed both cross a threshold — real drifting, not a canned animation.

Damage & wrecks

Hard impacts — walls, other cars, high-speed collisions — chip away a shared health bar for you and your ride. Hit zero and the car wrecks: it still rolls, but it's done as a getaway vehicle.

Headlights, brakes, smoke

Headlight cones sweep the road at night, brake lights flare under hard stops, and a damaged engine trails exhaust smoke — sparks fly on every real impact.

Lane-disciplined traffic

NPC drivers keep to the right lane, brake for whatever's ahead of them, and yield to you on foot — they're not just wandering obstacles, they're following the same rules you are.

Enter, exit, borrow

Walk up to any car and press E to take it — parked or moving. A watching pedestrian calls it in immediately; an empty street means a clean getaway.

The garage remembers

Park your current car at the garage tile for two seconds and its rolled stats, paint and health save to this browser — the same car is waiting near your spawn point on your next run.

The physics, in plain terms.

  • Throttle and brake accelerate or reverse the car; letting off coasts it down with natural drag.
  • Steering authority scales with speed — you can't spin the wheel like a go-kart at highway speed.
  • Velocity chases where the car is pointed, at a rate set by grip — the handbrake cuts that grip so momentum and heading disagree, which is what a drift is.
  • Impacts above a speed threshold convert directly into damage and a hard bounce-back, not a wall you simply stop against.

The honest footnote: it's an arcade model tuned for feel, not a simulation — the same trade-off most top-down driving games make.
